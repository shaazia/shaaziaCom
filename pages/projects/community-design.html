Case Study

Online Communities & Conversation Platforms

I designed community and conversation experiences for large brands—creating scalable information architecture, interaction patterns, and moderation-ready UI systems that turn support and product discussion into healthy, high-signal communities.

Community UX Conversation Design Information Architecture Wireframes Personas Enterprise Scale

What I worked on

Across these engagements, the consistent challenge was the same: design a platform that supports thousands (or millions) of asynchronous conversations while staying readable, searchable, and safe to participate in. The work emphasized clear hierarchy, predictable navigation, and tools that enable moderation and community health at scale.

My design focus: reduce “conversation friction” (finding answers, asking questions, contributing value) while increasing trust (credibility cues, moderation clarity, and transparent norms).
  • Information architecture: categories, boards, thread structure, search, and “find answers fast” patterns
  • Conversation UX: posting, replying, solutions/accepted answers, sorting, and participation cues
  • Community health: moderation affordances, governance cues, and incentive/reputation mechanics
  • Personas: power users vs. newcomers, and the needs that drive contribution

Client

AT&T

Wireframes for enterprise community experiences focused on discoverability, thread readability, and support workflows. The design emphasized scannable lists, strong hierarchy, and fast paths to “resolved” content without overwhelming users.

Client

Nike

Community hub UI concepts designed to encourage participation and highlight valuable contributions. The experience balances brand storytelling with practical community utilities—support, ideas, and social engagement—so users can both connect and self-serve.

Client

Autodesk

Annotated wireframes and component guidance for a large-scale community landing experience—designed for findability, repeatable modules, and predictable navigation across forums, product areas, and support destinations.

Client

Macy’s

Community and content experiences designed around engagement loops—making it easy to discover stories, ask questions, and return for new posts. The work emphasized readability, content hierarchy, and participation prompts.

Client

Micron

Persona work to clarify audience motivations, constraints, and contribution behaviors—used to inform IA, tone, and the “why participate” mechanics that sustain a healthy community over time.

Client

Google Glass

Persona and UI design for early wearable community experiences, prioritizing glanceable information, low cognitive load, and privacy-aware interaction patterns. The work explored how community support and discussion translates to a new form factor.

Client

Infiniti

Wireframes and personas supporting an automotive community and in-vehicle experience context—balancing feature richness with safety and attention constraints. The work separated primary driving-critical behaviors from secondary exploration and content.

Outcome

Across brands and domains, the deliverables established consistent patterns for findability, thread readability, and participation—supporting self-service, reducing friction in getting answers, and creating healthier community dynamics at scale.

Project snapshot

  • Focus: Online communities
  • Work: IA, wireframes, personas, UI
  • Scale: Enterprise platforms
  • Domains: Retail, telecom, software, wearables, automotive

Design themes

  • Findability & search-first IA
  • Readable thread patterns
  • Healthy participation loops
  • Moderation readiness
  • Incentives & credibility cues
Pattern I repeatedly used: make the path from “I have a problem” → “I found the answer” shorter, and the path from “I can help” → “I contributed value” clearer.
Typical artifacts: annotated wireframes, component guidance, IA maps, persona frameworks, and end-to-end conversation journeys (browse → post → resolve → return).
