Case Study

Prime Video Live Events Help Hub

A redesign of the Prime Video Live Events Help Hub—built to help customers get access, sign in, and troubleshoot live streaming issues faster while maintaining series branding (Thursday Night Football, NASCAR, and more).
Live site

UX Design Content Strategy Information Architecture Help & Support UX Live Streaming

Situation

Live events are time-sensitive: customers often arrive minutes before kickoff and need immediate help with access, sign-in, device compatibility, and streaming performance. During Thursday Night Football in particular, these moments drove high support contact volume.

The existing Help Hub page supported branded series experiences, but it wasn’t optimized for the live-event job-to-be-done: “Help me start watching (or fix what’s broken) right now.”

Live-event support has a different bar: customers aren’t browsing—they’re trying to watch right now.

Task

Redesign the Live Events Help Hub to reduce decision load and route customers to resolution faster—while preserving series branding and introducing Amazon Customer Service support cues alongside Prime Video.

Primary UX problems to solve: choice overload, mental model mismatches, redundancy, and an information hierarchy that didn’t match urgency.

Action

I evaluated the existing page structure and redesigned it around a help-first hierarchy and task-based information architecture. The approach focused on four improvements: (1) faster orientation, (2) predictable navigation, (3) scannable “How to watch” steps, and (4) progressive disclosure to reduce redundancy and choice overload.

Screens labeled Before (existing) reflect the original experience. Screens labeled After (my redesign) show the concept redesign work I created.

Action 1: Help-first hierarchy

The original experience leaned heavily into a promotional hero, competing with the customer’s urgent goal. I reframed the top of the page to establish purpose immediately and route customers into “How to watch” as the primary path.

Action 2: Clear, predictable navigation

Customers expect support pages to behave like support: clear sections, consistent labels, and fewer competing entry points. The redesign standardizes navigation around a task-based IA aligned to user intent (How to watch / Where to watch / Issues / FAQs / Get help).

Action 3: Scannable chunking for “How to watch”

“How to watch” is the critical path. I condensed the steps into a single scannable module: get the app (if needed), sign in / confirm access, find the event, and take action (Watch now / View schedule).

Action 4: Reduce redundancy and choice overload

The original page repeated links and pathways across sections, creating a “which one do I click?” moment. I shifted the design toward progressive disclosure: surface the most common actions first, push edge cases deeper, and tighten CTAs so they read as support—not marketing.

Result

This concept redesign establishes a scalable pattern for branded Live Events help pages: help-first hierarchy, consistent task-based IA, scannable chunking for the critical path, and progressive disclosure for troubleshooting and FAQs—while keeping series identity intact and adding clear support cues.

Project snapshot

  • Product: Prime Video Live Events Help Hub
  • Context: Branded series pages (TNF, NASCAR, etc.)
  • Users: Customers trying to watch live events
  • Focus: Access, sign-in, streaming troubleshooting
  • Artifacts: IA, page structure, content modules

Design themes

  • Match customer mental models
  • Chunk content for scanning
  • Reduce choice overload
  • Remove redundancy
  • Keep branding, signal support
What this redesign improves: a faster “start watching” path, clearer navigation, less decision fatigue, and a consistent support experience across branded live event series.
