Customer Obsession

Customer Obsession means starting with real customer needs—especially moments of confusion, friction, or failure—and working backward to design experiences that reduce effort, increase clarity, and restore trust. It goes beyond listening to feedback; it requires deeply understanding customer intent, constraints, and emotional state, then making principled tradeoffs in service of long-term customer value, even when systems, policies, or org structures are complex. Customer obsession shows up in the details: how errors are explained, how choices are simplified, and how customers are guided toward successful outcomes rather than left to navigate complexity on their own.

Examples in my work

Across my portfolio, I’ve demonstrated this by designing help-first, intent-driven experiences in high-stakes environments. In the Prime Video Live Events Help Hub, I restructured information architecture and content to reduce choice overload and guide customers quickly to “start watching,” prioritizing resolution over internal taxonomy. In Amazon Customer Service work across chat, voice, and devices, I focused on trust-building patterns—clear explanations, safe defaults, and strong recovery paths—so customers understood what was happening and what to do next, especially when AI systems were involved. In each case, decisions were grounded in customer mental models and moments of need, ensuring that even complex systems felt understandable, supportive, and human.

The design goal: reduce steps and cognitive load while increasing transaction confidence—so customers can complete tasks quickly, correctly, and without needing support.

The challenge

The existing experience made simple tasks feel complex. Common workflows (transfers, bill pay, account inquiries) required too many screens, unclear labels, and inconsistent information hierarchy—leading to abandoned attempts and increased support contacts.

Approach

I redesigned the mobile banking experience around task-based navigation, consistent UI patterns, and transaction transparency. The work balanced usability with compliance and security expectations by making key details easy to verify before committing.

  • Task-based IA: prioritize what customers do most, not what the org structure suggests
  • Clear hierarchy: “what matters now” vs. “details when needed”
  • Trust signals: confirmations, review states, and predictable error recovery
  • Spec-ready design: patterns documented to scale implementation across teams

Artifacts

The deliverables included UI layouts, detailed workflow specs, and supporting strategy and research assets used to align cross-functional partners and accelerate delivery.

Design specs: mobile workflows

Documented end-to-end flows and screen-level specifications to reduce implementation ambiguity, improve consistency, and support iteration based on real customer behavior.

UX strategy: where mobile fits in the ecosystem

Banking isn’t a single channel experience. I mapped how customers move between self-service, branch touchpoints, and assisted support to identify high-value moments where mobile can reduce friction and improve outcomes.

Personas: designing for real banking behaviors

Personas grounded the work in real customer constraints—technology comfort levels, banking habits, and pain points— ensuring the product served both confident mobile users and customers who need extra clarity and reassurance.

Process flow: account opening complexity

Account opening is a high-friction journey with many compliance and verification steps. I created process flows to make complexity explicit, align stakeholders on decision points, and identify opportunities to reduce drop-off.

Outcome

This work produced a clearer, more scalable mobile banking foundation—streamlined workflows, stronger information hierarchy, and reusable specifications that improved consistency and helped teams ship with less ambiguity.
